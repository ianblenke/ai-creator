## Narration (what is spoken)

The plan names the facts your thesis depends on, and the fact sheet is where each one earns its place. The rule is simple: the script and the scenes may take a fact only from this one file, and every fact carries a marker. A cited fact has a public source, and you note the link and the day you read it. A measured fact comes from your own test, so you note how, when, and how many runs. An estimated fact is your own best guess: you note what it rests on, and the script says about. A marker is only as good as the reading behind it, so read the source itself, and write the claim no stronger than the source is. A search snippet, a forum post or a model's answer is a lead, not a source.

## Visual intent

- A sheet with three rows, each with a small tag: a link tag for cited, a ruler tag for measured, a tilde tag for estimated. A magnifier opens a source page beside the cited row, and a tag that says 'no stronger than the source' slides under it. A snippet, a forum post and a speech bubble fade in dim at the edge, marked 'a lead'.
