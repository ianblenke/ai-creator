## Narration (what is spoken)

Smooth bridges cannot tell you whether a claim is true, so the first reviewer, the critic, checks exactly that. The critic is an AI model that you ask to attack what the script claims. It gets three things: the script, the fact sheet, and the plan if you wrote one. The fact sheet is the list of facts, with sources, that the script is allowed to use. Every claim needs a fact behind it, because a wrong claim is the thing you cannot take back once it is public, and nothing may be stated more strongly than its source. The critic also looks for anything private, a machine, an address or a person's name, because anything said in the video is public the moment it goes out. It ends by giving the thesis as it heard it, and a verdict: ready to record, or fix first. If an AI helped you write the script, start a new conversation for the critic, because a writer cannot see its own gaps. It can only read, so it reports problems instead of quietly rewriting your words.

## Visual intent

- The same script page now beside a stack of three small pages labelled by shape only: facts, plan, script. A reviewer figure with a magnifier and no pencil circles a bold claim bar in magenta. At the end a thesis bar appears beneath it, then a verdict stamp reads fix first.
