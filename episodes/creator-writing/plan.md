# Episode plan: Writing for the Ear

## Thesis
A script that reads fine on a page can sound like a list of facts to a listener, so before you record, two read-only reviewers listen for you: a flow review that finds abrupt changes of subject, and a critic that attacks claims and structure.

## Audience
Someone making their own narrated video who has a script and suspects it sounds flat or jumpy. They arrive with the question: why does my script read well and sound bad?

## Not covered
The voice and its recording checks (later part). Pictures and scenes. The law and the licence for cloning a voice. Choosing a topic (the plan part).

## Facts needed
- What each prompt checks and what it is given (the repo's two prompt files).
- The order and how they are run (the repo's agent-layer doc).
- The repo's writing guidelines (script format doc).
- Where the flow review came from: a viewer comment, told as process, no names.

## Scene sketch
1. Hook: two made-up versions of one sentence about a kettle, a list of facts and a reason.
2. This part's route, because the hook raises how to get from one to the other.
3. A listener cannot see headings, because that is why a list sounds worse than it reads.
4. What a bridge is, because the missing bridge is the fix.
5. One story per scene and a reason for each fact, because bridges alone do not stop a list.
6. The critic, because a smooth script can still be wrong.
7. The flow review, because a bridge problem is found by listening, not by the writer.
8. Running them, because the order matters.
9. What they cannot catch, because a model reads text.
10. Close and next part.

## Risks
- Accuracy: say nothing about how many problems the reviewers find; say only what the prompts ask for. The kettle sentences are made up and said to be made up.
- Disclosure: no names of people, places or machines; the viewer comment is paraphrased.
- Rights: none.

## Done when
- `validate`, `safety`, the critic and the flow review pass (no open HIGH or MEDIUM).
